# Overhead Shoulder Press Technique and Biomechanics Guide
**Source:** NSCA Guidelines & Saeterbakken & Fimland (2013) Biomechanical Evaluation
**Citation:** Saeterbakken, A. H., & Fimland, M. S. (2013). Effects of body position and loading modality on muscle activity and strength in shoulder presses. Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research, 27(7), 1824-1831.

## 1. Biomechanics of the Overhead Shoulder Press
The overhead shoulder press is a compound multi-joint vertical pushing exercise targeting the anterior and lateral deltoids, clavicular pectoralis major, triceps brachii, and trapezius.
Key joint angles:
- **Starting / Lowered Rack Phase:** Dumbbells or barbell held at collarbone / clavicle height. Elbows bent at approximately 90° (DOWN_THRESHOLD ≤ 95°). Wrists stacked vertically above elbows.
- **Overhead Extension / Up Phase:** Arms press vertically until elbows reach full lockout at 155° to 180° overhead (UP_THRESHOLD ≥ 155°). Wrists are directly above the shoulder joint center.
- **Back Alignment:** Spine must maintain a neutral lordotic curve (back angle ≥ 160°).

## 2. Common Form Errors
### Error A: Excessive Lumbar Arching ("Excessive Arch")
- **Biomechanical Cause:** User leans the upper torso backward, hyperextending the lumbar spine (back angle < 140°).
- **Root Causes:** Weak abdominal stabilizers, insufficient thoracic spine mobility, or attempting to turn the vertical press into an incline bench press using the larger sternal pectoralis major.
- **Risk:** High facet joint compression, disc shear strain, and lower back injury.
- **Correction:** Squeeze glutes firmly, pull the ribcage down, and tuck the pelvis. If shoulder mobility is limited, reduce the load or use a high-incline seated variation.

### Error B: Incomplete Overhead Lockout ("Incomplete Extension")
- **Biomechanical Cause:** Failing to press past 130°-140°, reversing direction before full vertical extension.
- **Correction:** Press the dumbbells straight upward until upper arms are aligned beside the ears. Ensure wrists stay stacked over elbows.

### Error C: Flaring Elbows Outward Beyond the Scapular Plane
- **Correction:** Keep elbows slightly forward in the scapular plane (roughly 30° forward of the coronal plane) to reduce impingement on the supraspinatus tendon.

## 3. Recommended Camera Setup for Shoulder Press
- **Angle:** Front-facing (frontal) or slight 3/4 oblique view.
- **Distance:** 2.0 to 3.0 meters away.
- **Overhead Framing Clearance:** Camera must be tilted high enough so that when arms are fully locked overhead, the hands and wrists do not clip out of the top edge of the frame.

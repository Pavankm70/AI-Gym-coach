# Forward and Walking Lunge Technique and Balance Guide
**Source:** American Council on Exercise (ACE) Exercise Science & Riemann et al. (2012)
**Citation:** Riemann, B. L., et al. (2012). Biomechanical comparison of forward and lateral lunges. Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research, 26(9), 2314-2321.

## 1. Biomechanics of the Lunge
The lunge is a unilateral compound lower-body movement targeting the quadriceps, gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and calves while challenging core and pelvic lateral stability.
Key joint angles:
- **Standing / Up Phase:** Front and rear legs extended at 160° to 180°.
- **Bottom Lunge Phase:** Front knee bends to ≤ 100° (ideally 90° tibiofemoral angle). Rear knee lowers directly toward the floor to approximately 90° without crashing into the mat.
- **Torso Alignment:** Torso remains upright (torso angle ≥ 160° relative to vertical).

## 2. Common Form Errors
### Error A: Lateral Loss of Balance ("OFF BALANCE")
- **Biomechanical Cause:** Excessive lateral deviation of the center of mass (offset > 0.10 normalized units between shoulder midpoint and hip midpoint).
- **Root Causes:** Stepping in a straight line ("tightrope walking") rather than stepping along parallel tracks (railroad tracks). Weak hip abductors (gluteus medius).
- **Correction:** Step forward while maintaining a hip-width base of support. Look forward and engage core stabilizers.

### Error B: Front Knee Over-Extending Forward of the Toes
- **Biomechanical Cause:** Excessive forward tibial shear causing high patellofemoral compressive force.
- **Correction:** Step farther forward and direct descent vertically downward rather than driving forward.

### Error C: Torso Leaning Forward
- **Correction:** Keep chest high, shoulders down and back, and core braced.

## 3. Recommended Camera Setup for Lunges
- **Angle:** Side (sagittal) view or 45-degree angle.
- **Distance:** 2.5 to 3.5 meters.
- **Framing:** Full body framing showing both feet on the floor and full torso upright.

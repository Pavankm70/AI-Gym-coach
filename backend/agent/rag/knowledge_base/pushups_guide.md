# Push-Up Technique, Biomechanics and Core Stability Guide
**Source:** American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) Exercise Guidelines & Contreras Biomechanical Studies (2012)
**Citation:** Contreras, B., et al. (2012). The Biomechanics of the Push-up: Analysis of Joint Angles and Muscle Activation. Strength & Conditioning Journal, 34(5), 41-46.

## 1. Biomechanics of the Push-Up
The standard push-up is a closed-kinetic-chain horizontal pushing exercise activating the pectoralis major, anterior deltoids, triceps brachii, and rectus abdominis.
Key joint angles:
- **Plank / Up Phase:** Elbow angle is 160° to 180° (arms extended without joint jamming). The body line from shoulders to hips to ankles forms a rigid straight line (body angle > 160°).
- **Bottom / Chest Phase:** Elbow angle decreases to ≤ 90° as the chest approaches 2-3 inches from the ground.

## 2. Common Form Errors
### Error A: Hip Sagging (Lumbar Hyperextension)
- **Biomechanical Cause:** The pelvis dips toward the floor (hip deviation > +0.08 normalized units), breaking the straight spinal alignment.
- **Root Causes:** Weak transverse abdominis and gluteal muscles failing to resist lumbar extension under gravitational load.
- **Risk:** Increases compressive shear load on lumbar vertebrae L4-S1.
- **Correction:** Squeeze glutes and pull the belly button inward toward the spine; maintain a posterior pelvic tilt throughout the set.

### Error B: Piked Hips (Hips Too High)
- **Biomechanical Cause:** The hips rise significantly above the shoulder-ankle line (hip deviation < -0.08 normalized units).
- **Root Causes:** Compensatory mechanism to reduce core load by shortening the lever arm.
- **Correction:** Lower the hips into a true neutral plank before initiating the descent.

### Error C: Elbow Flare
- **Biomechanical Cause:** Flaring elbows out at 90° relative to the torso.
- **Risk:** Increases subacromial impingement risk in the rotator cuff.
- **Correction:** Keep elbows tucked at a 45° to 60° angle relative to the ribcage ("arrowhead" shape, not a "T" shape).

## 3. Recommended Camera Setup for Push-Ups
- **Angle:** Side (sagittal) view, placed at floor level or low tripod height (0.5 to 1 meter).
- **Distance:** 2.0 to 3.0 meters away.
- **Framing:** Full body length from head to heels must fit horizontally in the camera frame.
